THE FOUNDER'S LETTER

I built the practice
I wanted to see.

Why I started The Alert Room, and who I hope it helps.

“Access to learning should not decide who gets to grow.”

ERIK YEGHINYAN · FOUNDER & CREATOR

Why I started

I kept coming back to a simple problem: so many people want to build a career in anti-money laundering and financial-crime prevention, but getting practical training can be difficult. Some courses and certifications are expensive. Others are hard to access. And even when you find the right material, there is still a gap between learning the terminology and knowing what to do when an alert is assigned to you.

For an entry-level analyst, that gap can feel enormous. You may understand KYC or recognize a red flag, yet still wonder how to investigate several accounts, which records matter, how to distinguish normal customer behavior from a meaningful concern, or how to write a defensible case narrative.

What my experience taught me

I have worked across AML and compliance investigations, transaction monitoring, fraud-related reviews and frontline banking operations. Those experiences taught me that the best investigations rarely come from jumping to a conclusion. They come from asking better questions, reviewing activity in context and documenting why a decision makes sense.

I drew on the work I have done, the professional education I have pursued and the training I have taken to shape this project. I am not presenting The Alert Room as an employer training system, a regulatory authority or a substitute for professional qualifications.

What I wanted to create

I wanted a place where someone could spend part of an evening learning a concept and then practice it in a way that feels closer to the work itself. Instead of reviewing only one neat example, you should be able to open a queue, prioritize different alerts, look at customer profiles and transactions, collect evidence, choose a disposition and explain your decision.

That also means learning to close a case when the evidence supports an ordinary explanation. Not every alert deserves escalation. A thoughtful analyst should be able to explain both what concerns them and what does not.

Why it is free

I created The Alert Room to encourage other analysts to develop their skills in their own time. Whether someone is just starting out or hoping to grow into a more advanced role, I want practical exercises to feel more accessible, not further out of reach.

The platform uses invented customers and transactions, not confidential records from any employer. The educational material is founder-directed and should continue to improve through review, primary sources and community corrections. Automated QA is transparently labeled as training feedback; human judgment remains the learner's responsibility.

Where it goes from here

This is a growing project. I want analysts to challenge it, suggest missing topics and tell me where an exercise could be clearer or more realistic. My aim is to build a learning space that respects the complexity of the job and the people trying to enter it.

Thank you for giving it a try. If it helps you ask one better question during your next investigation, then it is doing something worthwhile.

Learn the work. Practice the judgment.

Erik Yeghinyan

Founder & Creator, The Alert Room
